- Concept: the Panic of 1907. With no central bank, a run on New York's thinly reserved trust companies (about 5% cash against about 25% at national banks) froze the money market (call money to 100%), and J. P. Morgan organised the rescue privately, ending with the trust company presidents locked in his library on 3 November until they signed pledges. Our point of view: a rescue by many hands, run by one man who chose who was saved and whose firm did business on the way; the case for a public lender of last resort and the Federal Reserve Act of 1913. Original analysis; no advice.
