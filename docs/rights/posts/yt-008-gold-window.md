- Concept: the closing of the gold window on 15 August 1971. Bretton Woods (1944) fixed currencies to the dollar and the dollar to gold at $35 an ounce; more dollars ended up abroad than gold to pay them (the Triffin dilemma; IMF 1966 figures); gold at $40 in London in 1960; the run of August 1971; Camp David; the address; the Smithsonian agreement and its collapse by 1973. Our point of view: ordinary Americans had not been able to swap dollars for gold since 1934, so 1971 changed the dollar's anchor, not the cash in their wallets. Original analysis; no advice.
