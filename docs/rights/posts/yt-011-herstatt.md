- Concept: Herstatt, 26 June 1974. German authorities closed Bankhaus Herstatt at 3:30 p.m. German time; banks that had paid it Deutsche marks earlier that day for dollars due in New York were exposed for the full value when its New York correspondent suspended its dollar payments. The film explains why a currency trade settles as two payments in two countries, the five-hour gap, the run on payments that followed (New York's gross funds down an estimated 60% in three days; Coombs's "virtually freezing up the clearing process"), the Basel Committee (end 1974, first met February 1975), the CPSS (1990), CLS and payment versus payment (September 2002), and the BIS's 2026 measures: more than $14tn settled a day in April 2025, $5.2tn PvP, more than $1.4tn (10%) gross bilateral and fully exposed. Our point of view: a trade has two risks, the price and the clock; the cure was a clock, not a forecast. Original analysis; no advice.
