- Concept: Long-Term Capital Management, 1998. A fund run in part by Robert Merton and Myron Scholes (Nobel 1997) loses $1.8bn in August 1998 and more than half its capital since January; three weeks later fourteen banks and securities firms, called together by the New York Fed, put in about $3.6bn for 90% of the fund. The film explains the convergence trade (two near-identical Treasuries, a gap seldom much more than a tenth of a percentage point), why it needed borrowed money, what more than 25 to 1 leverage does when prices move the wrong way, the flight to quality after Russia's default of 17 August, the four days in September, and why the Fed got involved with no public money (McDonough, Greenspan), the moral hazard it raised and the PWG's finding on credit terms. Our point of view: leverage doesn't change whether you're right, it changes how long you can afford to be wrong. Original analysis; no advice.
