- Concept: Charles Ponzi, Boston, 1919 to 1920. His flyer promised fifty percent profit in forty five days and the price gap he pointed to (an international reply coupon bought for 30 centavos in Spain, swapped for a 5-cent US stamp) was real; the film shows why it could never pay: about 10 percent in theory, paid in stamps, and even free coupons would have needed at least 95,840,000 of them to cover the $4,792,000 gap the Supreme Court's figures imply. Then the Boston Post, the run, the 1924 ruling ("equality is equity"), the SEC's definition, Madoff and the DOJ's victim fund, and the coupon's retirement at the end of 2026. Our point of view: a real, small mechanism made an impossible promise sound checkable. Original analysis; no advice.
