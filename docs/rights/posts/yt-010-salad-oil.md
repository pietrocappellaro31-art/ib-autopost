- Concept: the salad oil swindle of 1963. Tino De Angelis's Allied Crude Vegetable Oil Refining leased 139 tanks in Bayonne, New Jersey; lenders held warehouse receipts for 1.8 billion pounds of vegetable oil and scarcely 100 million pounds were found. The film explains field warehousing and warehouse receipts as collateral, the tricks Time described in 1965 (a weighted tape and "90% full", water topped by a thin slick of oil, oil pumped between tanks on weekends, forged receipts), the futures bet and its margin, the crash of 15 November 1963, Ira Haupt's suspension and the stock exchange's $12 million and trust in the week of President Kennedy's assassination, the claims on American Express, the 20-year sentence, Norman Miller's Pulitzer, Buffett's partnership buying American Express, and SIPA 1970. Our point of view: collateral is a chain of trust in which each link trusted the one before, and the borrower paid for the check. Original analysis; no advice.
