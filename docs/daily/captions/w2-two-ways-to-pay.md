Same company, bought for $100 and sold for $150. Why does one buyer make 1.5x and the other 2.25x?

Top: all your own money. You put in $100, you get $150 back. 150 / 100 = 1.5x.

Bottom: you put in $40 and borrow $60. At the sale you repay the $60 first, so you keep $90. 90 / 40 = 2.25x.

The company didn't change. The gain was $50 both times. Debt just means you put in less of your own money, so the same gain is a bigger multiple on it. That's the whole idea behind an LBO.

It cuts both ways though. Sell for $80 instead and the top buyer gets 0.8x, the bottom one gets $20 back on $40, 0.5x. I left out interest on the debt to keep it to one line; it would trim the 2.25x a bit.

Which buyer would you rather be? Tell me in the comments.
