You own 80% of a company. So why is all of its EBITDA sitting in your numbers?

Because once you own more than half, you control it, and control means you report the whole thing. Your own business makes $40 of EBITDA, the subsidiary makes $10, so you show $50. Not $48.

Here's the catch. Your share price only pays for your 80%. The other 20%, worth $20 in this example, belongs to outside owners. That's minority interest (you'll also hear noncontrolling interest).

So the bridge goes: $300 of equity, plus $100 of debt, minus $20 of cash, plus $20 of minority interest. Enterprise value is $400, and $400 / $50 is 8x.

Forget the minority piece and you'd get 7.6x. The company would look cheaper than it really is, because your EBITDA covers owners your EV left out.
