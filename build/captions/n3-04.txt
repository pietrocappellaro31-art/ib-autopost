You spend $100. Capitalise it or expense it? Same cash out the door, very different-looking numbers.

Take it pre-tax, year 1, with a 5-year life.

Expense it and EBITDA drops 100. So does operating cash flow.

Capitalise it and EBITDA doesn't move. You book 20 of depreciation, add it back, and operating cash flow ends at 0, while the $100 shows up in investing as capex.

That's why analysts always check the policy. It flatters EBITDA.

Free cash flow (CFO - capex) is -100 either way. If 2 companies treat it differently, you'd compare FCF, not EBITDA.
