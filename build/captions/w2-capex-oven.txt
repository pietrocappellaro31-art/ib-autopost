She spends $100 on an oven. Does her profit fall by $100? No, and you'd be surprised how often this one comes up in accounting interviews.

Her bakery sells $150 of bread a year. Flour, wages and rent cost $100, so a normal year makes her $50.

If you count the whole oven in year 1, profit looks like -$50. But she'll use that oven for five years, so it goes on the balance sheet and you spread it out: $20 a year of depreciation. Year 1 profit is $30.

Cash is a different story. The $100 left her account on day one, so her cash is down $50 this year.

Add up all five years and both come to $150. Same money, different timing. That's why you'll always want the income statement and the cash flow statement side by side.
