You own 10% of a company. Then it sells new shares and you own 8%. Did you just lose money?

Nope.

Say it's worth $1,000: 100 shares at $10. You hold 10 of them, so 10%, worth $100.

It sells 25 new shares at $10 and takes in $250, so now there are 125 shares, your 10 are 8% of them, and the company itself is worth $1,250.

Divide by 125. Still $10 a share.

Your 10 shares? Still $100.

The catch is price. Sell those 25 at $6 and only $150 comes in, which leaves $1,150 over 125 shares, $9.20 each, and your stake falls to $92.

A smaller slice of a bigger pie can be worth exactly the same. What decides it is the price the new shares go for.
