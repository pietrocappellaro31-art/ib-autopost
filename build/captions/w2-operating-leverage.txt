Sales up 10%. Profit up 60%. Same company, same year.

The trick is fixed costs. Say a company sells $100: $40 goes on costs that move with sales, $50 on rent and salaries, and $10 is left as profit.

Sales rise 10% to $110. The moving costs rise to $44, rent and salaries stay at $50, so profit is $16. That's 60% more. Run it the other way and a 10% drop in sales takes profit down 60%, to $4.

That's operating leverage. It's why two companies with the same sales growth can report very different profit growth.
