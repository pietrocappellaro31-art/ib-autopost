You pay 2%, then 3%. Is that up 1% or up 50%?

Both.

Borrow $1,000 at 2% a year and you pay $20 of interest, but at 3% you pay $30, so the same move in the rate shows up as two different numbers depending on what you compare.

3 - 2 = 1. One percentage point.

$10 extra on $20? Half again. 50%.

Bankers dodge the mix-up by saying points, or basis points. One point is 100 basis points, so 2% to 3% is "up 100 bps".

Subtract two percentages and you get points. Divide the change by where you started and you get percent.
