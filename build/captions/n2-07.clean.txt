A customer pays you $120 upfront for a year. Is that $120 of revenue? Not on day 1.

Your cash goes up $120 and so does deferred revenue, which is a liability. You owe them 12 months of service.

Each month you deliver, $10 moves from deferred revenue to revenue. The cash came in once.

On the cash flow statement, you add the increase in deferred revenue back in operations, since the cash arrived before the revenue did.

Is it debt? Usually not. It's settled by delivering the service, so it sits in working capital and stays out of your EV bridge.

Save it for the accounting questions.
