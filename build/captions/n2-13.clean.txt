Goodwill is impaired by $50. Before you answer, say your tax assumption out loud.

If it isn't deductible, net income falls by the full $50. You add the 50 back on the cash flow statement, so cash doesn't move at all, and goodwill and retained earnings both drop 50.

Balanced.

Now say it is deductible at 25%. It works like depreciation: tax falls $12.50, net income falls $37.50, and you end up with $12.50 more cash.

Under IFRS and US GAAP for public companies, goodwill isn't amortised. It's tested for impairment.

Save it for the accounting round.
