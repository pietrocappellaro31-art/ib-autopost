$10,000 at 8% a year. How long until it doubles?

Most people divide: 8% of $10,000 is $800 a year, so they say 12.5 years. That's too slow. It's about 9.

The 8% isn't paid on the first $10,000 forever. It's paid on whatever's in the account that year, and that keeps growing. Year 1 adds $800. Year 2 adds $864, because the interest from year 1 earns interest too. So each year adds a bit more than the last.

After 9 years: $10,000 x 1.08^9 = $19,990. Just under double.

The interview shortcut is the rule of 72: divide 72 by the rate. 72 / 8 = 9 years. It's an approximation, but it's close at normal rates and you can do it in your head.
