A $30 profit this month, and her cash is down $70. Both are true at once, and it's why profitable businesses can still run out of money.

Mia prints $100 of posters for a client who'll pay in 60 days. She pays $70 for paper and ink today.

The sale counts when the work is done, so this month shows $100 of revenue and $70 of costs. Profit: $30.

But nothing has come in yet and $70 has gone out. Cash: down $70.

The missing $100 sits on her balance sheet as a receivable, money she's owed. When the client pays two months later, her cash ends up $30 higher, the same $30 as the profit, just later.

Profit counts what you've earned. Cash counts what's come in. Grow fast on credit and you can be profitable and still run dry.
