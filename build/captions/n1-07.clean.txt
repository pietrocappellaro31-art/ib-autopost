In a DCF, the terminal value is usually the biggest single number. Interviewers know it, so they'll push on it.

Perpetuity growth: final-year FCF times (1 + g), over WACC minus g. Keep g below long-run economic growth, or you've built a company that eventually becomes bigger than the economy.

Exit multiple: year 5 EBITDA times a multiple from today's comps.

Then use each to check the other.

Your growth rate implies a multiple, and your multiple implies a growth rate, so if 1 of them looks absurd you know which assumption to fix.

Last step, and people forget it: TV sits at the end of year 5, so you discount it 5 years.

Save this for DCF questions.
