DESCRIPTION:
A safe $100 bond pays 1.5% a year for ten years. Then new bonds start paying 4%. Yours can't default, so why won't anyone pay $100 for it?

Each year it pays $2.50 less than a new bond. Over ten years that's $25, and because later money is worth less today, the gap is worth about $20.28 now. So the bond is worth $79.72, and at that price it earns 4% a year, the same as a new one. The bond is our own example and the maths works in any currency.

How we worked it: $1.50 a year for 10 years plus $100 at the end, each payment discounted at 4%. It's our own arithmetic, with no outside data in this Short.
