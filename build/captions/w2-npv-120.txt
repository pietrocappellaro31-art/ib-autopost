You put in $100 and get $120 back two years later. That can still be a bad deal, and it's one of the first things a banker checks.

Say the same money could earn 10% a year somewhere else, at the same risk. After one year that's $110. In year two you earn 10% of $110, so $11, and you're at $121.

The deal pays $120. Your other option pays $121. You'd be $1 behind.

Bankers flip it around: what's the $120 worth today? Divide by 1.1 twice and you get about $99.17. Pay $100 for that and the net present value is negative.

Getting more back isn't enough. It has to beat what the money could earn elsewhere.
