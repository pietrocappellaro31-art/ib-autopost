Why is there no P/EBITDA? 1 rule answers that, and every other multiple question you'll get.

The numerator and the denominator have to belong to the same people.

Enterprise value is the whole business, so it pairs with anything measured before lenders are paid. That's 4 of them: EV/Revenue, EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, and EV over unlevered free cash flow.

Equity value is the shareholders' slice, so it pairs with what's left after lenders. That's 3: P/E, price to book, and price over levered free cash flow.

P/EBITDA mixes them. Price is after debt. EBITDA is before it. The ratio describes nobody.

Here's why EV/EBITDA does the heavy lifting. Take 2 identical businesses. Company A has no debt and trades at a P/E of 20. Company B is heavily levered and trades at 12. Same operations, same 100 of EBITDA, and you'd conclude B is 40% cheaper. Their EV/EBITDA is identical, because enterprise value and EBITDA both sit above the capital structure.

Then say what EBITDA hides. Capex, working capital and cash taxes, all 3 ignored. For a capital-intensive business, EV/EBIT is often fairer.
