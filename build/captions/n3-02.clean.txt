You sell equipment with a book value of $50 for $60. The trap here is counting the cash twice.

The $10 gain is profit. At 25% tax, net income rises $7.50.

On the cash flow statement you take the gain back out of operations, because it isn't operating cash. Operations ends at -2.5, which is just the tax you paid. The full $60 goes into investing. Cash is up $57.50.

Balance sheet: cash +57.5, PP&E -50 (book value, not the price), retained earnings +7.5. Balances.

Save it for your accounting round.
