Beta 2 means a stock swings twice as much as the market. Twice the return, then? Nope.

Say safe government bonds pay 4% and the market's expected to return 9%, so you'd think a stock that moves twice as hard should get you 18%. That's the myth.

Beta only doubles the part above the safe rate. That part is 9% minus 4%, so 5%, and twice 5% is 10%. Put the 4% back on and you land at 14%, a full four points under the 18% you might have guessed.

Expected return = safe rate + beta x the market's extra. That's CAPM. It's how bankers usually get the cost of equity for a DCF.
