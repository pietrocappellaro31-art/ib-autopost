Depreciation goes up by $10. You'll probably get the income statement right, and then lose it on cash.

Pre-tax income drops $10. At a 25% tax rate you pay $2.50 less tax, so net income falls $7.50, not $10.

On the cash flow statement you start from that -7.5 and add the 10 back, because depreciation is an accounting charge and not a single dollar of it actually left the bank. Cash ends up $2.50 higher.

That's the tax you saved.

Balance sheet: PP&E down 10, cash up 2.5, assets down 7.5. Retained earnings down 7.5. It balances.

If yours doesn't, check the tax line first.
