Asset deal or stock deal. Same company, same price, very different tax bill.

In a stock deal you're buying the whole entity, and every liability comes with it. Lawsuits too. The tax basis just carries over.

In an asset deal you pick what you take, and the tax basis steps up to the price you paid. Pay $500 for assets with a $200 tax basis and you've got $300 more to depreciate for tax, so you pay less cash tax for years.

So buyers usually want assets. At a 25% tax rate, that extra $300 of deductions is worth up to $75 in tax saved over the years it's written off.

Sellers usually want stock. In the US, a C-corp that sells its assets can get taxed twice: once on the gain, again when the cash goes out to shareholders.

Save it for your M&A round.
