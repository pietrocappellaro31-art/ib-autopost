Is stock-based compensation a real expense? "It's non-cash" is only half the answer.

It hits the income statement, but no cash leaves, so you add it back in operating cash flow.

The cost shows up somewhere else. Say you own 1% of a company with 100 million shares. It issues 2 million to staff, and your 1 million shares are now about 0.98% of it.

That's the cost. Dilution.

In valuation, count it once: either treat it as a cash cost and cut free cash flow, or keep it added back and put the future dilution in your diluted share count.

Doing neither is the mistake I'd look for.

Save this before a tech or growth company interview.
