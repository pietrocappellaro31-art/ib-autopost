TITLE: $100 of Bonds, $4 of Your Own. They Fall 4%. What's Left?
ALT 1: Prices Fell Just 4%. Why Was All Your Money Gone?
ALT 2: A 1% Drop Wipes Out a Quarter of Your Money. How?

DESCRIPTION:
You buy $100 of bonds with $4 of your own and borrow the other $96. Then the bonds fall 4%. What's left of your $4? Nothing. 4% of $100 is $4, and the loan still wants all $96 back, so your stake is gone. At 25 to 1 even a 1% fall takes a quarter of it.

This happened. In 1998 the hedge fund Long-Term Capital Management ran at more than 25 to 1, lost 44% of its capital in August alone, and weeks later 14 banks and securities firms put in about $3.6 billion to keep it from collapsing. The $100 example is ours, not one of the fund's trades.
