$120 comes in, and January's revenue is just $10. Where did the rest go?

A gym (made up for this one) sells a year's pass for $120, paid on 1 January, which works out at $10 for each month of gym time it now has to deliver.

Cash: all $120, day one.

Revenue: only what's delivered. So $10.

The other $110 sits on the balance sheet as deferred revenue. It's a liability. The gym still owes you 11 months of workouts, and every month $10 of that moves out of the liability and into revenue until, by December, the liability is $0 and revenue has reached the full $120.

Cash counts when it's paid. Revenue counts when it's earned.
