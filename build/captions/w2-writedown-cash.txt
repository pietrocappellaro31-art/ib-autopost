$10 of inventory gets written off and profit falls $7.50. So why is cash up $2.50?

Leo's shop (made up for this one) has $10 of winter jackets nobody will buy, so he writes them down to $0.

Income statement: it's a $10 expense, so profit before tax falls $10. At a 25% tax rate the tax bill's $2.50 lower, and net income falls $7.50.

Cash flow: no cash left the shop, since he'd paid for the jackets long ago. Start from the -$7.50, add back the $10 that wasn't cash, and cash ends up $2.50 higher than it would have been.

Balance sheet: inventory down $10, cash up $2.50, so assets fall $7.50. Retained earnings fall $7.50 too. It balances.

The $2.50 is just the tax he doesn't pay: $10 x 25%.
