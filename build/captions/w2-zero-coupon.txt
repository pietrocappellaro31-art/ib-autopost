It pays 0% interest. So why does anyone buy this bond? Simple: nobody pays $100 for it.

Say it pays you $100 back in two years and nothing before that, while other bonds like it pay 5% a year. Then it's worth about $90.70 today. That's $100 divided by 1.05, twice.

Hold it two years and your $90.70 turns into $100. The $9.30 in between? That's your interest. It still works out to 5% a year, you just get it all at the end instead of along the way.

So a zero coupon bond always sells below face value. The gap is the interest, and when rates go up, it gets bigger.
