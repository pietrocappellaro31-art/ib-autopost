"Which method gives the highest valuation?" There's a usual order, and the reason matters far more than the order does.

Precedents usually print highest. Comps sit below. The DCF ranges widest of the 4. An LBO sets the floor.

Why precedents beat comps.

A comp is what 1 share trades at, a minority stake with no say in anything. A precedent is what somebody actually paid to own all 100% of it and run it. That price carries a control premium, often 20 to 30%, plus whatever synergies the buyer talked themselves into.

Why the DCF ranges widest. WACC, terminal growth and your year 5 margin are 3 numbers nobody can verify, and a 1% move in any of them swings the answer more than a revenue assumption you'd argue about for a week.

Why the LBO is the floor. A sponsor needs its target return, so there's a hard ceiling on what it can pay. A strategic buyer with synergies goes higher.

Then say which one you'd lean on. Liquid comps, lean on comps. Live sale process, lean on precedents. Stable forecastable business, the DCF earns its place.

Say typically. Then say why.
